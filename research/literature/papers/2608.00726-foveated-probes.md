# Foveated Probes Recover Localized Binding Information in Vision Foundation Models

## Source, version and reading scope

Michalkiewicz, Baktashmotlagh and Balakrishnan, [arXiv 2608.00726v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00726v1), 2026-08-01. Checked 2026-09-17: selected [methods, task construction, results and appendix comparisons](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00726v1). No local replication.

## Tested setting and observations

Frozen encoders feed separately trained readouts/probes. Main SigLIP synthetic paired-counterfactual accuracy is 3.5% for global averaging, 93.5% for foveation and 99.3% for the region oracle. Natural GQA paired accuracy is 4.3%, 17.0% and 33.0%, respectively. Natural foveation closes about 45% of the summary-to-oracle gap. The learned selector receives task supervision; the oracle receives an annotated target region. Counterfactual nuisance-to-signal measurements compare target-changing and target-preserving distractor edits.

## Authors' interpretation versus our assessment

The authors argue that global readout can conceal localized information. Our assessment: this refutes an inference from pooled-readout failure directly to absent information. Recovery of most oracle-accessible performance applies to the synthetic comparison; it should not be transferred wholesale to natural images. A successful trained probe supplies a witness of recoverability under its supervision and interface, not a description of native generative computation.

## Limits and strongest alternative

Our strongest alternative for local transfer is that an added selector learns task-specific access that the original model never acquired. The contrast can diagnose a readout restriction while leaving additional representation and policy failures open. Question-conditioned selection and annotation-assisted selection also supply different information. Neither establishes autonomous enumeration, owner coverage or stopping. Representation sensitivity alone is not a measure of causal necessity for the original output.
