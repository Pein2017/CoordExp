# Gaze Heads: How VLMs Look at What They Describe

## Source, version and reading scope

Gandikota and Bau, [arXiv 2606.14703v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14703v1), 2026-06-12. Checked 2026-09-17: selected methods/results in [sections 3–6 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14703v1), including controls and evaluation scope. No local replication.

## Tested setting and observations

On Qwen3-VL-8B, heads selected by query-dependent panel attention also track narration. Redirecting 100 heads reaches 83.1% target-panel accuracy on 3,000 held-out strip–target pairs; random non-gaze controls from layers 20–35 reach 14.6% (matched layer range, not necessarily exact per-layer counts). Region steering transfers to COCO category naming. Evaluation uses an LLM judge. The target region is externally supplied. During schedule changes, textual panel numbering can remain unchanged while narrated content follows the intervention.

## Authors' interpretation versus our assessment

The authors interpret these heads as a dynamic gaze mechanism. Our assessment: this is evidence for a causal spatial steering surface, with a useful separation between visual content and textual progression. It does not by itself identify the process that chooses a new physical instance in native generation. Discovery of an actuator and discovery of an autonomous selection policy are different research outcomes.

## Limits and strongest alternative

Main evidence concerns six-panel narration; some tested model families lack comparable heads. Our strongest alternative for local transfer is that forcing visual attention supplies the missing selection decision without explaining its natural origin. Spatial steering may succeed even when coverage memory, geometry binding or stopping remains defective. A next-token or category effect must therefore retain its own evidence scope.
