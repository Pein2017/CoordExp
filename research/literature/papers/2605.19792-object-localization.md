## Tested setting and observations

LLaVA-1.5 and InternVL-3.5 are tested on filtered COCO, retaining only one instance per queried category. Probing additionally selects cases whose classification responds to object removal. Duplicating object tokens into surrounding positions expands predicted boxes. Internal-object shuffling barely affects LLaVA localization, but Table 2 reports InternVL falling from 72.64 to 37.70. Head mediation and ablation identify task-sensitive subsets.

## Authors' interpretation versus our assessment

The authors propose containerization: object-related tokens collectively determine extent. Our assessment: this is a useful intervention-supported hypothesis, not proof that explicit coordinate representations are absent. The arXiv-v1 table materially limits the prose claim that internal arrangement is mostly irrelevant across architectures. Preserve the model-specific counterexample when reusing that explanation.

## Limits and strongest alternative

Our strongest alternative is that duplicated semantic evidence at new positions changes localization through several possible computations; extent following alone does not identify a unique internal representation. Filtering removes the same-category ambiguity central to our owner-selection problem. A prompted target class with one eligible instance does not test autonomous selection among repeated instances. Sparse-head effects can identify a causal dependency without exhausting the computation or proving a universal sequential architecture.
