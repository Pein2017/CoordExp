# Soft Prompting for Few-Shot Adaptation of Vision-Language Models

## Source and reading scope

Gare et al., **Your Model Already Knows Don't Teach It, Learn to Ask It: Soft Prompting for Few-Shot Adaptation of Vision-Language Models**, [arXiv:2609.11310v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11310v1), 2026-09-10. Checked 2026-09-17: selected method, experiment disclosures and results, especially §§4.1–4.4. No local replication.

## Tested conditions and observations

The backbone is frozen; one to three continuous prompt tokens are learned at the image/text boundary. In 10-shot Roboflow20-VL detection, the reported mean 7,168 prompt parameters match the best tested LoRA configuration at 14.2 mAP. Main numbers are single runs; the paper discloses substantial seed variability. Medical-domain performance remains poor.

In §4.3, domain prompts are **not supplied off-domain**. Unchanged off-domain behavior therefore follows from using the unchanged backbone. On LVIS Rare 50, prompt length also matters: the reported three-token configuration falls below the unadapted baseline.

## Authors' interpretation and our assessment

The authors argue that specialized knowledge can often be accessed through small prompts. Our narrower reading: low-dimensional input adaptation is a useful comparator to weight updates. Success does not identify a unique access mechanism; added supervision and changed computation remain part of the intervention.

## Limits and alternatives

Removing a prompt outside its domain does not establish preservation while that prompt is active. Neither frozen parameters nor cross-task reversibility guarantees retaining old physical instances in the same detection output. Prompt placement, class information, optimization and output calibration can contribute without proving all missing-instance information was already usable.
