# Capacity and readout: what has actually been demonstrated?

**Question:** does the model lack usable finite-task capacity, or do the chosen constraints, objective and update fail to realize it? Read [vocabulary](../glossary.md) before treating all occurrences of Source, N16 or DoRA as the same object.

The old `identity-generalization` filename must not change that same-panel estimand.

Output-head surgery is not necessary merely to fit this exact panel. Magnitude-only and full-adapter learning are different surfaces; do not transplant a successful learning rate by name.

The transfer results are different evidence. ## Mature untied package and effective coordinate readout

[Fixed-state readout accounting](../experiments/2026-09-18-untied-active-readout-geometry/results.md) separates norm and alignment contributions at selected native slots. Equal norms reverse a subset of endpoint wins; most survive, and affine logit slope alone also fails to explain every endpoint winner. [Gradient accounting](../experiments/2026-09-18-untied-gradient-path-accounting/results.md) remains technical-invalid; its repaired axis-value bug does not explain the separate finite-difference CE discrepancy. No current gradient or training-origin inference is admitted.

## Supported, not promoted

A shared network can jointly realize at least those finite annotated tasks. That defeats a blanket impossibility argument. It does not show that useful features are equally accessible on all images, that a learned correction transfers, or that arbitrary scenes are physically complete. Captured teacher states, free states and fitted parameters are different objects.

Small residual norm can reflect compatible structure or image/prefix-specific addressing. Norm depends on constraint deficits and geometry; semantic names do not determine it. Changing selected output rows also changes the parameter space, so historical norm values cannot silently become a fixed-surface scaling theorem.

## Magnitude-matched direction control

The [accepted direction comparison](../experiments/2026-09-19-readout-direction-control/results.md) shows that reflected or fixed balanced-sign score changes can also reduce recurrence on selected failure prefixes. At identical states each coordinate receives the same absolute FP64 score change, so magnitude alone does not identify norm correction as the unique repair. Some random controls retain useful norm directions; all directions also have adverse cases. This leaves shared local corrections and path perturbation as alternatives, without negating empty-prefix population measurements or identifying a training defect. The observed first0→1 association on donut routes is tested separately, not promoted as a mechanism.

## Structured coordinate inputs do not imply either uniform stability or uniform chaos

The [accepted input-continuity audit](../experiments/2026-09-19-coordinate-input-continuity/results.md) reconstructs the actual `natural_adjacent` initializer exactly: shared projected linear/Fourier features, not independent random vectors per coordinate bin. Its highest-band adjacent phase is about 0.805 radians, so a smooth formula need not vary slowly or have distance monotonic in coordinate separation. Trained input rows and effective output rows are measured separately; the untied package has distinct input/readout geometry. No initialization or training-origin cause follows from those distances alone.

At 16 selected fixed native histories, all 32 admitted +/-1 input contrasts modify y2. Only one immediate next-row x1 winner changes and none at the fixed later x1; input perturbations and full-vocabulary logit changes are nonetheless measurable. The intervening suffix is forced native, so this is neither a test of x1 input changes nor a free-feedback stability result. Existing sensitive free branches and mostly stable fixed-prefix winners can coexist because a small-margin first argmax change can alter later context. The [spatial](../experiments/2026-09-19-recurrence-spatial-source/results.md) and [conditional-mass](../experiments/2026-09-19-recurrence-conditional-mass/results.md) results add context dependence without establishing a unique readout or internal recurrence mechanism. No architecture edit, initialization replacement or training is released from this package.

## Phase/role continuation distinguishes token stability from recurrence

The [accepted phase/role continuation](../experiments/2026-09-19-recurrence-phase-decision/lead-results.md) extends the preceding all-y2 fixed-suffix audit to symmetric x1/y2 edits and free releases. Only4/352 initial opener/EOS choices change, but120 trajectories diverge,114 first at coordinates. Most first divergent complete-row pairs remain numerically close (78/114 same-description, maximum coordinate difference<=8); this is not a physical-identity judgment. Two-row native token bridges do not eliminate every later effect, and coordinate sensitivity does not consistently suppress repetition. These results neither vindicate globally smooth embeddings nor identify a training/initialization defect. No embedding rewrite or new training follows; the unit is closed with its6/16 first-ever subgroup correction and archival limits explicit.
