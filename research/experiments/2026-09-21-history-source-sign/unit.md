## Frozen question

From an owner-grounded mature-model native boundary, does recomputing the prefix while blocking one historical owner's source row from all successor attention paths change finite A/C/N competition and actual next-row reselection selectively?

The target failure boundary and separately matched non-revisit control are interpreted independently. A source cut is the net effect of removing that row's direct and relayed attention paths while retaining literal tokens, positions, image, model and decoding policy. It does not isolate abstract coveredness from spatial, visual or contextual content.

## Intervention and evidence

For a source row `[start,end)`, the barrier blocks target queries beginning at the native `box_end` query `end-1` from attending source-content keys `[start,end-1)`. Full-prefix recomputation applies the barrier to all successors, including relay positions. The `box_end` token is unchanged, so its residual anchor is content-independent under the barrier.

Qualification requires source-trace decision parity, causal-4D no-op full-vector parity within `2e-4`, legal same-length replacement invariance within `2e-4` at multiple downstream logits, and rejection of the leaky `q>=end` construction. Scientific cells retain per-row full-vocabulary log probabilities, finite-set contrasts, first-fork competition, one barrier-active free row, and at most eight rebuilt original-model rows.
