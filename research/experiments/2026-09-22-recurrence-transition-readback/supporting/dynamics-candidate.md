The anchor's repeated x1 token has signed margins `+0.0675735, +0.0135803, +0.0138474, +0.0108757` on rows 16–19, then `−0.0288334` at the row-20 x1 fork. It does not decline monotonically even before the fork. In those same four identical rows, x2's fixed-rival margin increases from `+0.01877` to `+0.27824`; the exact repetition therefore does not imply uniform depletion across coordinate roles. Its x1 runner-up changes twice, so the x1 top-two gap is also a changing-rival envelope rather than one fixed-pair trajectory.

The mature examples provide stronger counterexamples to monotone confidence accounts. In `val:7511` rows 27–88, the repeated x1 winner and runner-up remain token IDs 151670 and 151671 throughout; its margin spans `0.96859–1.46184`, rises overall, and changes slope sign 26, 26 and 12 times using descriptive deadbands `1e-4`, `1e-3` and `1e-2`. Rows 51–54 alone give `1.204813, 1.225586, 1.201393, 1.216904` (each alternating step at least `0.01551`). The invalid capped `train:351017` fixed x1 pair has margin range `1.16128–2.05956` and 150, 144, 95 turns at the same deadbands; rows 127–130 give `1.701794, 1.724762, 1.698236, 1.724731` (each alternating step at least `0.02297`). These deadbands are descriptive sensitivity checks, not certified replay-error bounds.

Under the **conditional** mapping of a stationary scalar two-real-decay response `d_n=d_inf+A*lambda^n+B*rho^n` with `0<lambda,rho<1` directly to one fixed winner-versus-runner-up logit margin, first differences have at most one sign reversal. The fixed-pair x1 windows materially weaken that literal mapping. They do not reject history mechanisms generally: logits may depend on nonstationary context, interactions and other coordinates. Later coordinate roles after an earlier fork have different conditioning, so their scores are not common-prefix causal contrasts. The strongest live alternatives remain position-dependent competition, coordinate drift on one physical instance, and distinct mechanisms behind superficially similar repetition. Visual ownership is a separate candidate review; numerical exit, EOS and cap have no owner verdict here. No forecast or timescale fit was made.
