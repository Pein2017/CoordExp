## Candidate question

At a naturally reached repeated-row boundary, does the previous row's coordinate
input have unusually strong directional influence on the next repeated-versus-new
decision margin, compared with matched perturbations and a healthy native boundary?
For an admitted next-coordinate contrast, the local quantity is
`(u_repeat - u_new)^T J_prefix delta_e`.
The model, actual history and readout remain fixed within each local contrast.
This measures local influence; it does not establish a stable attractor, owner
ledger, training-origin mechanism, or a useful free-generation repair.

The strongest alternative is generic sensitivity to changing any recent input,
including off-distribution embedding interpolation, rather than recurrence-specific
feedback. Matched direction controls and a healthy boundary are therefore required.
Tying alone does not determine the sign or size of this influence.
The new model also has axis loss, so cross-package differences cannot isolate tying.

temperature/RP search, new images, or unregistered long continuations.
