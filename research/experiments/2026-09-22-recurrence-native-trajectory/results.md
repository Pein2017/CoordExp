## Conditional first-layer computation

Both cases have exactly constant layer0 pre-Q at the declared coordinate role,
per-role pre-K/V throughout the repeated rows, and query residual input. Despite
this, layer0 residual output varies: maximum elementwise first-query difference
is0.326185 in val and
0.089953 in train. Contextual differences
therefore already arise during the first block, before any repeated-row
first-layer K/V content variation is available.

With identical templates, the complete repeated pool can be indexed by relative
age d. In exact arithmetic its unnormalized score mass is
`Z_h(N)=sum_(d=1..N,j) exp(s_hj(d))`; advancing the query and newest row together
preserves recent-age terms and adds the oldest relative-age term. This is a
computation-level consequence, not a claim about an absolute-position clock.
Fixed pre-run/image/prompt keys age relative to the query, so their scores and
the normalized attention can still vary nonmonotonically.

## Lead judgment and next frontier

The most useful current explanation is an unchanged token template repeatedly
read through a changing relative-position and history composition, producing
contextual state trajectories and a changing multi-candidate competition.
A rapidly settled representation with only a late categorical switch is weakened
by this assay. A scalar learned counter is neither required by these observations
nor excluded as one component. The earlier donor and phase interventions retain
their local causal scope; this temporal assay adds descriptive structure.

The next distinct question is how much first-layer state evolution comes from
fixed image/prefix anchors aging relative to the query versus the growing
repeated pool. Exact first-layer attention decomposition is a cheap preliminary
contrast, but it must not be promoted to final-output causality. A new model
intervention would need a separately frozen contrast and acceptance criterion;
no additional model arm was launched in this unit.
