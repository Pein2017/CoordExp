# Matched-Objective Coordinate-Branch Signature Comparison

## Decision

The comparison is complete. Do not start the proposed complete-row consistency
training screen on the Gaussian-smoothed coordinate and Ranked Probability
Score checkpoint. Use the matched pure cross-entropy plus token-type-gate
checkpoint as the primary baseline for the next mechanism unit.

The full outcome, evidence tables, artifact paths, limitations, and stopping
decision are recorded in `results.md`.

The final execution-path control is also complete: the Gaussian checkpoint was
sampled for the same six images and ninety-six ordered seeds with the exact
current sampler used by pure cross-entropy. All trajectories closed naturally,
all first-seed replay checks passed, and no prediction was dropped. The bowl
endpoint spread and one dense-person branch failure remained; the historical
bus-wide spread narrowed to a stable checkpoint-specific left-boundary shift.
This preserves the decision above while removing the sampler implementation as
the primary explanation.

The preceding six-case study used a checkpoint trained with Gaussian-smoothed
coordinate targets and a Ranked Probability Score term. Its distributed
probability around physically valid boundaries may therefore be a consequence
of that objective rather than a general property of Qwen3-VL coordinate
generation. The first control replays the exact same fixed states with the
matched pure cross-entropy plus token-type-gate checkpoint at step 4887.

That fixed-prefix control is necessary but not sufficient. The source prefixes
were sampled from the Gaussian-smoothed checkpoint, and several are very
unlikely under pure cross-entropy. Before any training treatment, the unit must
therefore complete the missing native-trajectory comparison: sample both
checkpoints under the same prompt, policy, image set, and seeds; admit branch
pairs using the same physical-owner rules; and score admitted prefixes under
both checkpoints.

## Question

Given the same image and generation conditions, which coordinate-branch
signatures survive both a fixed-prefix checkpoint intervention and a change in
the checkpoint that natively produced the trajectory?

The fixed-prefix arm is a local conditional comparison. It does not claim that
Gaussian-checkpoint prefixes are natural states under pure cross-entropy. The
native-trajectory arm is required to estimate whether the observed failure is
actually reachable under each checkpoint's own policy.

## Compared checkpoints

1. **Gaussian-smoothed coordinate objective.** The existing step-4887 DoRA
   adapter trained with ordinary row cross-entropy, a coordinate-token Gaussian
   target, a Ranked Probability Score term, and a token-type gate.
2. **Pure cross-entropy objective.** The matched geometry-sorted step-4887 DoRA
   adapter trained with ordinary cross-entropy and a token-type gate, without
   the Gaussian or Ranked Probability Score coordinate terms.

Both checkpoints use the same Qwen3-VL 2B base model, description-first row
format, geometry-sorted supervision, adapter rank, effective training batch
size, and nominal training horizon.

## Frozen evidence

- Reuse the six crop-reviewed cases from
  `../2026-07-18-fixed-prompt-clean-versus-degraded-coordinate-branch-replication/cases.json`.
- Preserve each source pair's image, exact prompt token identifiers, category,
  row-zero coordinate trajectories, and accepted reference extent.
- Keep the three primary same-owner extent cases separate from the three
  same-class owner-switch controls.
- Use the source model's bfloat16 forward plus float32 probability arithmetic
  as the primary numerical comparison and a full-model float32 forward as a
  precision-sensitivity control.
- Run a separate unconstrained greedy row with repetition penalty 1.0 for the
  pure cross-entropy checkpoint. This greedy run measures natural behavior;
  the fixed-prefix replay measures the distribution at held-constant states.

## Explicit counterfactual contract

The source rollout bundles were generated by the Gaussian-smoothed checkpoint.
Replaying their prefixes with the pure cross-entropy checkpoint is an explicit
counterfactual intervention. The scorer must therefore:

1. retain strict equality checks between the clean and degraded source
   bundles;
2. retain all image, prompt, history-policy, row, category, and token-prefix
   checks;
3. require an explicit opt-in when the executed model composition differs from
   the source bundles;
4. record both source and executed model compositions in the result artifact;
5. never describe this replay as a natural pure cross-entropy rollout.

## Native-trajectory factorial

Use the same six reviewed images, exact base prompt, sampling temperature 0.4,
top-p 0.95, repetition penalty 1.0, generation horizon, and seed list for both
checkpoints. Keep the generated token identifiers and parsed rows for every
attempt, including malformed and naturally closed trajectories.

The complete comparison has four cells:

| Prefix source | Gaussian-smoothed scorer | Pure cross-entropy scorer |
|---|---|---|
| Gaussian-smoothed native trajectory | required | required counterfactual replay |
| Pure cross-entropy native trajectory | required counterfactual replay | required |

Report denominators before selected examples: sampled trajectories, valid
rows, category-matched rows, same-owner candidate pairs, owner-switch pairs,
and pairs rejected by physical review or admissibility checks. Do not select
only the largest coordinate shifts.

For every admitted branch pair, report source-token rank, full coordinate-path
log probability, accepted-boundary mass before and after the sampling policy,
later-coordinate distribution shift, final box coherence, and whether the pair
represents one physical object or a change of owner.

## Predictions that distinguish explanations

### Shared autoregressive geometry behavior

If both checkpoints show broad raw probability near a valid boundary, strong
competition between nearby coordinate modes, and branch-dependent changes at
later coordinates, then the earlier result is not merely induced by Gaussian
smoothing. A later treatment should target coherent full-row decisions or
branch stability rather than adding more local coordinate smoothing.

### Objective-induced distributed support

If the Gaussian-smoothed checkpoint uniquely assigns substantial raw mass to
valid coordinate neighborhoods while the pure cross-entropy checkpoint is
sharper and more isolated, then the Gaussian and Ranked Probability Score
terms successfully reshape local support. If the source top-p policy removes
that support anyway, the next treatment should address the mismatch between
training-time distribution shaping and inference-time selection.

### Smoothing harms decisive localization

If the pure cross-entropy checkpoint has stronger correct-coordinate modes,
less later-slot drift, and more coherent greedy boxes, then stronger smoothing
is not the default next step. The existing loss may trade local probability
coverage for weaker decisive geometry.

### Both checkpoints lack valid support

If both checkpoints assign negligible support to the physical boundary in the
same cases, a coordinate-only calibration screen has weak motivation. The next
unit should instead examine object selection, prefix state, or visual binding.

## Measurements

For every coordinate slot under both clean and degraded prefixes, compare:

- the coordinate-vocabulary argmax;
- the probability and rank of the sampled clean and degraded tokens;
- raw temperature-one probability within 4, 8, 16, and 32 coordinate bins of
  the accepted physical boundary;
- the same neighborhood after the source temperature 0.4 and top-p 0.95
  policy transform;
- coordinate entropy and the difference between clean-prefix and
  degraded-prefix distributions;
- the unconstrained greedy row and whether its phrase and four boundaries form
  one coherent physical object.

The primary cases and owner-switch controls must not be pooled into one rate.

## Confounders

- The two checkpoints differ in learned parameters, not only in one isolated
  loss term; this experiment identifies a training signature, not a single
  causal gradient term.
- The fixed prefixes came from the Gaussian-smoothed checkpoint and may be
  off-distribution for pure cross-entropy. The separate pure cross-entropy
  greedy run is required for that reason.
- Six hand-reviewed cases are sufficient for mechanism discrimination, not a
  population performance estimate.
- The accepted reference boxes can remain ontology-sensitive, especially the
  umbrella case.
- Bfloat16 and float32 forward passes can move tokens across a top-p boundary;
  forward precision and probability arithmetic precision must stay explicit.

## Stop condition

Close this unit after both precision arms, matched natural greedy runs, and all
four native-trajectory/scoring cells are compared, followed by one written
decision:

- launch a narrowly specified training screen;
- revise the candidate training objective; or
- stop coordinate-focused training and return to selection, prefix state, or
  visual binding.

Do not launch training merely because implementation is available.

If hard cross-entropy is coherent on its own trajectories while the
Gaussian-smoothed objective is not, reduce or redesign the smoothing objective
before adding a new consistency loss. If both objectives show the same
on-policy branch failure, a small matched training screen is justified. If
each fails only on the other checkpoint's prefixes, classify the current
effect as checkpoint-specific off-policy mismatch and do not train against it.
