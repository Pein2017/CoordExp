# Compilation objective: separate action fitting, route access, and preservation

Status: **candidate independent advice**, 2026-09-14. Owner: compilation adviser.
No model execution, image review, implementation, or experiment authorization.
This first assessment was written without reading another adviser's note.

## Bottom line

**Do not yet make either reconstructed dose trajectories or prefix refresh the
main explanatory experiment. First use the available endpoints to locate the
failed implication on a small, fixed set of witnessed tasks.** My confidence is
high in the logical distinctions and moderate in this priority.

The shortest useful ladder is: exact registered history -> freely chosen next
row and suffix; then the same history with the target supplied -> free suffix;
compare with the already recorded root output. Start with unambiguous
single-target conditions. This determines whether the immediate problem is
conditional realization, downstream preservation, or access to a condition
whose useful behavior already works. It needs no new labels, temperatures,
optimizer, objective family, or reconstructed training run.

Pro's F/R comparison can subsequently estimate the value of **one selectively
admitted refresh algorithm**. It does not by itself identify history mismatch
as the cause of A/B failure. Nor does stronger complete-row supervision solve
the legitimate-branch problem automatically.

## 1. What is actually being compiled?

Let `pi_theta` be the token policy and `G_theta(I; h, kappa, B)` its deterministic
greedy continuation, with exact supplied tokens `h`, cache state `kappa`, and
remaining allowance `B`. An unpatched deployment output is
`Y_theta = G_theta(I; empty, native, 3084)`.

For each image, retain a finite, independently trusted owner set `T`, incumbent
obligations `R subset T`, and proposed additions `N subset T`. Let `C_T(Y)` be
the owners actually present under the frozen matching/physical-review rules.
The task is about the set `C_T(Y_theta)`, not the identity of a particular
string or its place in the sequence. Atomic instances, groups, class/extent,
and annotated-owner metrics remain separate.

Four predicates should not be collapsed:

1. **Discovery:** a supplied/sampled/intervened trajectory contains a trusted
   target or useful route. This establishes an existence witness under its
   actual conditions, not under native conditions.
2. **Conditional realization:** from a specified unpatched `h`, *free* greedy
   produces the intended owner action, or a different verified useful action,
   with a useful complete continuation.
3. **Root compilation:** the same parameter checkpoint, from the original
   image and prompt without supplied history or cache changes, realizes the
   target benefit. It need not revisit the literal training history.
4. **Preservation:** the complete root output retains the registered incumbent
   owners and acceptable error burden; transfer is a further, separately
   sampled property.

For a supplied `h+c` continuation, `c` has **intervention credit**, not free
discovery credit. Report free-suffix owners separately. Running to EOS checks
the actual horizon; it does not certify all its content or scene completeness.

## 3. A newly visible legitimate-branch issue

Root reports a fresh projection of existing final-live positive scores and
input records, without model runs:

- 69 records represent **56 distinct image + exact-prefix conditions**:
  43 singleton conditions and 13 conditions with two distinct target rows.
- A has 20 complete literal target rows greedy; B has 19. At the distinct
  condition level, these are 20/56 and 19/56 conditions with any registered
  literal row greedy, not 20/69 and 19/69 independent achievable tasks.
- The old16 records occupy 11 conditions, with a registered literal greedy row
  at **all 11 conditions in both A and B**. Nevertheless old11-image root TP
  drops 101 -> 89/77. Old local next-row availability and old natural coverage
  loss therefore coexist; access versus post-entry damage remains unresolved.
- The new53 records occupy 45 conditions, with registered literal greedy rows
  at only 9/45 in A and 8/45 in B. Old and new tasks should not be pooled into
  one story of failed fitting.
- On the new53 records, B improves summed NLL and token hits versus A
  (369.4935 vs 376.3100; 411/492 vs 408/492), but has fewer complete literal
  rows greedy (8 vs 9).

This new evidence is **root-derived and attributed**, not independently
recomputed by this adviser. Its source is the `final_live_positive_scores` plus
the training-input records; root owns exact artifact binding in the synthesis.

At one exact context, two distinct rows diverge at some token. Deterministic
greedy cannot choose both at that fork. Thus a requirement of 69/69 literal
row realization would be internally impossible. Simultaneous eventual
coverage of two owners remains possible, but must use a successor condition,
not two different compulsory next rows at the same condition.

Conversely, collisions cannot explain away all the current fitting gap. A has
36 new conditions without a registered literal greedy row; only eight new
conditions have two rows. **At least 28 new singleton conditions** therefore
lack literal greedy realization (B: at least 29). An alternative good box,
owner, or route can still make some of these semantic successes. That is why
the next read should test actual behavior, not declare all 36 failures.

Before assigning blame to branch averaging, distinguish paired rows that are
different owners from same-owner coordinate aliases, and whether taking one
row already releases the other owner later. The collision itself proves an
evaluation/target-interpretation hazard, not the cause of transfer regression.

Root additionally reports that per-record CE coefficient is `1/2` for old
records but `8/53` for new records. The latter is `16/53` of the former;
equal 32 positive exposures do not mean equal coefficient-weighted target
pressure. This makes optimization allocation a live alternative for the new
condition gap. It does not establish actual Adam update ratios or causation:
gradient directions, clipping, optimizer moments, and protection also matter.

## 4. What the losses and margins can guarantee

For a row `c=(c_1,...,c_L)`, define

`m_t = z_theta(c_t | I,h,c_<t) - max_{v != c_t} z_theta(v | I,h,c_<t)`.

- Row CE is `-sum_t log pi_theta(c_t | I,h,c_<t)`. An improvement can all occur
  after the first failed fork. Even increasing complete-row probability does
  not force greedy to enter its first branch.
- The B hinge compares the vetted target token with **one repeat token**. It
  does not outrank another bad or unknown token, and a first fork does not
  identify a complete physical-owner event.
- Strictly positive `m_t` for every row token implies that the same execution
  starting from exactly `h` generates `c`. A tie needs the actual tie-breaking
  rule. This says nothing about reaching `h` from the root or retaining the
  suffix.
- Positive margins along every token of an entire root trajectory, including
  its ending, are sufficient for exact reproduction by elementary induction,
  assuming the same execution semantics. They are not necessary for equivalent
  owner-set success and should not become an exact-string deployment metric.

If several complete vetted routes share a prefix, represent their *logical*
allowed continuations as a small trie; no new runtime architecture is implied.
The policy may choose any child with a valid complete witness, then must stay
with that child's own continuation. Mixing positive coordinate slots across
different rows creates unsupported boxes.

Neither marginal route probability nor a sum-of-positive-child probability
guarantees greedy success: good children with probabilities .24/.23/.21 lose
top-1 to a bad child at .32. A margin between the highest viable child and the
highest **confirmed bad** child protects only against those known bad choices.
An unreviewed top child remains unknown. A full-vocabulary margin can force a
chosen string, but does so by outcompeting other valid and unknown alternatives;
it has no general preservation guarantee.

**Simple competing design, not yet a launch recommendation:** if collided
conditions are shown to be materially limiting, choose one fixed,
full-suffix-supported next-row witness per exact condition, keep the other
owners as eventual completion obligations, and use ordinary row CE with the
same aggregate condition weight. Compare this route choice against averaging
CE across alternative next rows; do not also refresh histories or add a new
hinge. Selection must be frozen before training, not chosen afterwards for
being the easiest learned route. This removes contradictory next-action
requirements without claiming that CE or one route solves root preservation.

## 5. Conditional KL: useful local protection, not a owner-set invariant

`KL(q(.|h,c,w_<t) || pi_theta(.|h,c,w_<t))` protects a distribution at that
precise registered condition. Even an exactly zero KL there cannot protect
an unseen root prefix. A small average KL is weaker still: a few low-margin,
behavior-critical positions can change while the average remains small.

A simple sufficient local condition is that every protected token probability
changes by less than half the teacher's top-1 probability gap. It is a
**per-position** condition along the actual path, not a consequence of the
reported mean KL alone. Even preserving all registered `w` rows leaves later
unregistered successors unconstrained.

Reusing `q(.|h,c)` as a target at `h'+c` is not preservation: it asserts an
unverified conditional invariance. Recomputing the teacher at `h'` avoids that
particular mismatch but can preserve a currently bad continuation. The existing
old-history protection bank may remain fixed for a clean F/R comparison, but
then it explicitly does not protect R's newly reached states.

For optimization, detach the frozen teacher, discrete candidate choice,
history selection, matching, and labels. **Do not detach the student's prefix
representations or reuse its detached training K/V** if the declared objective
is full sequence-conditioned DoRA learning. Recompute them with the current
parameters and allow ordinary gradients. Updating the sampled history outside
backprop is an alternating imitation procedure; it is not a policy-gradient
estimate of the root owner objective.

## 6. Unknown neutrality must have a precise meaning

Three different requirements are easy to conflate:

1. No unmatched/ambiguous prediction is adjudicated as a negative owner or
   assigned a negative reward merely because GT is incomplete.
2. A loss has zero direct derivative with respect to each unknown action's
   logit.
3. Training leaves every unknown action's probability/realization unchanged.

The current positive CE convention can satisfy (1), **not (2)**: for a
non-target vocabulary logit its derivative is `p_v`. A selected-logit pairwise
comparison can avoid direct unknown-logit terms, but shared-parameter updates
still do not guarantee (3). Ordinary KL likewise constrains unknown probability
mass without making it an annotated negative. The operational boundary should
remain (1), unless the user explicitly demands the stronger mathematical
condition; do not silently advertise CE as gradient-neutral to every unknown.

Rewarding a recognized owner is not permission to penalize an unrecognized
one. Scoring and reference protection also must not covertly convert unmatched
rows into EOS/negative targets.

## 7. What Pro's refresh comparison identifies—and misses

At common midpoint `theta_m`, let `A_i` indicate that task `i` remains unsolved
and its current-student history passes Pro's supplied-target full-suffix gate.
R trains on `h'_i` if `A_i=1`, otherwise on the original `h_i`; F retains all
original histories. If the frozen task pool, per-task weights, remaining
exposures, optimizer state, and final evaluation denominator are identical,
`Outcome(R)-Outcome(F)` estimates this **selective refresh policy's incremental
effect for this trained pair**.

Important qualifications:

- Selection using the *common pre-branch* midpoint does not automatically bias
  the full-denominator F/R comparison. It defines the algorithm. Bias appears
  if failed refreshes disappear from the denominator, accepted tasks are
  renormalized, or favorable final subsets/checkpoints are selected afterwards.
- Supplied-target acceptance selects states already downstream-repairable by
  the current model. It omits precisely the difficult states where the target
  can be entered but its continuation still needs learning. A null result
  cannot rule out history mismatch on those omitted states.
- Replacing histories changes context content, length, remaining budget,
  difficulty, gradients, and possibly meaning—not a scalar amount of
  “on-policyness.” A positive result supports the replacement rule; it does not
  prove that exposure bias caused the prior A/B regression.
- A fixed literal `c` may still describe a real unreported owner yet no longer
  be the appropriate *next* action after `h'`. Another route may preserve more
  owners, an equivalent row may already occur later, or too little budget may
  remain. Full-suffix checking helps, but only against its finite obligations.
- Keeping already solved tasks on their old histories avoids chasing harmless
  text changes, but continues training stale compulsory actions for the rest
  of the dose. This is a legitimate control choice, not evidence that mastery
  and preservation are handled.
- Once-refreshed histories can become stale again immediately. No DAgger-like
  general guarantee is being invoked here.

Root's fresh filesystem/source check finds final A/B adapters and a smoke
adapter, **not** A32/A128/B32/B128 or persisted optimizer states. Do not recreate
these by interpolation or rerunning the old experiment. Pro's future common
half-run plus full-optimizer clone is conceptually valid but is not an
available capability of the current save-after-loop trainer.

## 8. Preferred next question and finite stop

**Question:** on preselected existing trustworthy tasks, does A's failure occur
before a useful conditional next action, after that action, or only on the
root route? This is a bounded endpoint intervention contrast, not a new train
arm or an estimator of failure prevalence.

### Fixed design

- Use N16 and A only; A isolates the common learning failure. No need to repeat
  B's inferior increment to answer this question.
- Freeze at most eight existing singleton-prefix packages, four old and four
  new where available, with a deterministic selection rule and source-visible
  strata. Keep missing/inapplicable cases in the accounting; do not replenish
  until success. Use existing trusted targets and incumbent records.
- For each model and package, generate from exact `h` freely to the horizon,
  and from supplied `h+c` freely to the horizon: **at most 32 completions**.
  Reuse the current full-row score records and existing root outputs; do not
  rerun the 896-image evaluation. Matching must allow an already verified
  alternative owner row/extent, not only the registered literal string.
- Hold image, prompt, total3084 budget, parser, geometry, strict later-row
  repeat predicate, and owner obligations fixed. History tokens consume budget.
  Exact token/cache construction must be verifiable; comparing only decoded
  strings does not establish identical execution states.
- Primary evidence is a per-task ledger of target gain, retained/lost incumbents,
  autonomous suffix contribution, unknowns, and error burden; no pooled proxy
  score substitutes for it. Existing full physical blind32 remains pending and
  is not silently filled by this diagnostic.

### Decision-bearing outcomes

| Endpoint observation | Next decision changed |
| --- | --- |
| A cannot freely realize any vetted useful next action at exact `h`, but supplying `c` gives a preserved completion | Conditional entry is still unsolved; prioritize its actual update/competing-branch behavior, not claiming a pure access problem |
| Supplying `c` to A loses incumbents or produces bad completion, unlike N16 at the same condition | The conditional continuation changed; refresh gated on already-good suffixes cannot address the whole failure |
| A has a useful complete free continuation at exact `h`, but misses it from the root | There is a concrete access/path gap; a bounded selective F/R test now has a demonstrated target |
| Literal `c` fails, but a verified alternative gives the required owner set | The literal proxy was misleading; do not “repair” an already successful task |

Only in the third case, a supporting crossover may use the student's actual
root history `h'` on the same already frozen packages, against both checkpoints,
with and without supplied `c`—at most another 32 completions. Predefine the
boundary-selection rule before these reads; lack of a valid comparable
boundary is an outcome, not permission to hand-pick another route. This tests
whether the same witnessed repair remains useful at the new condition; it is
not required if the first ladder already resolves the immediate priority.

**Stop:** at 32 primary completions, or 64 including the conditional supporting
crossover; no added targets, temperatures, images, labels, losses, or training
seeds. Generated-token upper bounds are 98,688 and 197,376 respectively, before
deducting supplied-history budget; prefill and scoring are additional. Actual
wall cost must use the current measured runtime, not an invented throughput.
Future execution must use the user's detached tmux convention. This consultation
does not authorize it.

The strongest competing explanation that reverses this priority is that a
