# Pre-Onset Duplication Precursor Synthesis

## Overview

This document consolidates the June 12 pre-onset duplication precursor work. The thread asks whether coordinate-basin failure is visible before duplicate emission begins. The answer is yes, but with an important split: some pre-onset rows show easy probability drift, while the most mechanistically useful rows are rank-moving instability cases where masking, residual patching, or route/content interventions move a target coordinate between nearby basins. The strongest new bridge is Layer 16 head 8: score-bias alone is weak, key-state patching is not sufficient, but value-side and route/content patches show H8 can carry a causal pre-onset coordinate-basin effect in selected rows.

## From Precursor Drift To Rank-Moving Instability

The duplication-onset precursor panel established the first positive signal: selected duplicate windows can show coordinate-basin drift before visible duplicate emission. That made pre-onset probes worth running, but also showed attention concentration alone was not a sufficient explanation.

All-slot residual expansion preserved the main asymmetry but showed uneven payload. Worst damage was strongly masked-to-control-skewed (`13/16` rows), while best repair was mixed. The balanced rank-onset selector then separated the easy probability-drift surface from rank-moving/onset-instability rows. The selected rank-moving panel had many more causal rank shifts than the earlier probability-drift panel, making it a better target for mechanism work.

## Matched Pair And Residual Site Localization

The stratified selector chose a clean matched pair: a harmful-attractor row (`no_aligner_parent_ckpt3668:33:2:post_y1/pre_x2:-2`) and a useful-support row (`no_aligner_parent_ckpt3668:48:2:post_y1/pre_x2:-1`). The record-33 pilot showed that masking the duplicate-basin region improved the target coordinate from rank `5` to rank `2`, while patching control decoder-layer residual state back into the masked run restored the harmful control state at late layers. This is causal evidence that a local visual state can install a harmful residual direction before visible duplicate onset.

The matched-pair hidden-shift bridge showed that duplicate-basin pixel masking produces a hidden-state perturbation that grows through the decoder stack: record `33` rose from L2 `3.43` at layer `16` to `11.46` at `20`, `30.44` at `24`, and `123.88` by final state; record `48` showed a similar growth pattern but with a useful direction. Hidden-delta projection then split the cases directionally: record `33` became increasingly target-negative from layer `20` onward, while record `48` remained target-positive.

Rank-moving site localization found the strongest site/layer was often `mlp L16`: best repair was `mlp L16` in `7/11` rows and worst damage was `mlp L16` in `9/11` rows. This redirected the pre-onset branch toward Layer 16 residual/MLP boundaries rather than Layer 17/head-1 duplicate-basin routing alone.

## Layer 16 Head 8 Bridge

Layer 16 attention source routing identified heads `8`, `13`, and `12` as high-visual-mass heads on CTM/MTC positives, with visual mass dropping most strongly for heads `8` and `13` under duplicate-basin masking. Score-bias probes showed that L16H8 and L16H13 can move duplicate-basin attention mass but their mean coordinate-logit repair is small and inconsistent, so attention score movement is not sufficient.

Route/content probes resolved the H12/H8 ambiguity in favor of H8. L16H8 was not merely a visual-source attention sensor; it was the strongest CTM/MTC mover at the attention-output site. The strongest x2 bottle effect was specifically `value_delta_control_route`. Q/K origin probes then showed that, for the strongest `aux_latest_ckpt32 r79 row9 bottle x2` case, H8's score repair was key-side dominant (`key_delta_control_query=+1.576811`). But key-state patching alone remained a useful negative: it moved in the right direction only weakly. The value-side probe was strongest: value-source patching nearly reproduced the focal x2 bottle route/content repair.

## Consolidated Findings

- Pre-onset coordinate-basin drift exists before visible duplicate emission, but attention concentration alone does not explain it.
- Rank-moving selectors are more mechanistically useful than broad probability-drift selectors.
- The same duplicate-basin perturbation can become target-negative or target-positive depending on row/checkpoint context.
- Late residual vectors grow strongly after layer `16`, but the causal localization for selected rank-moving rows often points back to Layer 16 MLP/post-attention boundaries.
- L16H8 is the strongest current bridge head for selected pre-onset cases: score-only and key-only probes are insufficient, while value-side route/content patches carry causal movement.

## Decisions And Interpretation Boundaries

- Do not project the Layer 17/head-1 record-33 duplicate-basin mechanism onto every pre-onset row. Matched-pair route/content probes showed broader visual context and text-prefix state can dominate selected rows.
- Use balanced rank-moving selectors for future pre-onset mechanism probes; probability drift alone is too easy and too diffuse.
- Treat L16H8 as a bridge candidate, not a solved universal source. It is strong in selected x2 bottle cases and needs replication.
- Preserve both harmful-attractor and useful-support cases; the sign split is the mechanism, not noise.

## Unresolved Questions

- What determines whether the duplicate-basin perturbation becomes target-negative, as in record `33`, or target-positive, as in record `48`?
- Is Layer 16 head 8 a general pre-onset bridge or a selected-case route for x2/bottle-style failures?
- Can rank-moving selectors predict harmful versus useful residual direction before causal patching?
- How does the pre-onset L16/H8 route interact with the later Layer 17/head-1 route in visible duplicate bursts?

## Current Status

The pre-onset branch is now a separate mechanism surface rather than a footnote to post-onset duplication. It has evidence for pre-emission coordinate-basin drift, rank-moving residual causality, sign-split hidden deltas, and a Layer 16 head-8 value-side route/content bridge in selected rows. The branch is promising but not yet universal.

## Next Steps

- Replicate the L16H8 value-side route/content result on more balanced rank-moving cases.
- Build a small harmful-vs-useful sign classifier from history features, hidden-delta projections, and selector fields.
- Compare L16H8 bridge cases directly against Layer 17/head-1 post-onset cases to test whether they are sequential stages or separate mechanisms.
- Update this synthesis with future pre-onset probes instead of adding one report per selector/intervention slice.
