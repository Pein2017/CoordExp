# False-Negative Guidance And Coordinate-Basin Synthesis

## Overview

This document consolidates the June 11-12 false-negative guidance notes. The thread asks whether missed objects are truly invisible to the model or instead recoverable when the prefix, coordinate seed, or hidden state is changed. The consolidated answer is mixed but useful: many valid FNs are not pure visual misses; they have same-description neighbors, nearby visual proxies, or coordinate-prefix rescue paths. The strongest mechanistic example is the vase case, where full-prefix state locks the next `y1` coordinate into a wrong basin and same-slot late residual/MLP patching can repair it. The harder book and chair cases show negative controls and limits: some objects need broader context-band interventions rather than single visual-token or shallow same-slot patches.

## Guidance Probe Evolution

The initial decode smoke tested two FN cases across `desc_only`, `desc_x1`, and wrong-control tiers. It found that `desc_x1` could rescue at least one case while `desc_only` failed, proving that the object can exist in the conditional generative space even when normal rollout missed it. The wider prefix-depth and coord-slot panels expanded this into selected FN cases and showed that adding coordinate slots can create transition classes: some rows unlock at `desc_x1`, others need `desc_x1_y1` or `desc_x1_y1_x2`, and some remain hard no-rescue.

The joint visibility summary found that among valid measurable FNs, most were not clean "model could not see it" cases. About `71%` had same-description or nearby visual-proxy evidence. The auxiliary checkpoint had fewer valid FN cases and a larger share of language/proxy-evidence cases among valid FNs (`82.2%`), but also a higher invalid/parse-blocked share. This made FN diagnosis a binding and prefix-state problem as much as a raw visual-salience problem.

## Vase: Prefix-State Lock And Same-Slot Repair

The vase case became the cleanest hidden-state mechanism. At prompt end, empty-prefix `desc_x1` placed target `y1=468` near a plausible local basin (`rank=12`, top1 `472`), while full-prefix `desc_x1` locked onto the wrong vertical basin around `501` with rank `52`. Patching only the prompt-end late residual from the empty-prefix `desc_x1` condition into the full-prefix condition repaired the `y1` basin. Patch-site localization showed the strongest effect was late MLP-side, and the coordinate-direction decomposition found a two-step signature: a layer-26 target-local lift followed by layer-27 wrong-basin suppression.

Cross-slot negative controls were important. Cross-slot patching did not provide the same clean repair, so the durable target is same-next-slot state: source `0,desc_x1`, target `all,desc_x1`, next slot `y1`. The aux-vs-parent same-slot panel then split the mechanism into parent prefix-state lock-in, auxiliary shallow refinement, and cross-slot guardrails.

## Book And Chair: Harder Context-Band Cases

The book case resisted single visual-token patching. Visual-token probes around shared target tokens `[883, 922]` and lower neighbor token `[961]` were negative or weak, so the promising branch shifted to context rings and exact bands. Context-ring patches showed that upper/context row tokens, target-adjacent rows, and neighbor/lower rows affected the next-coordinate basin differently. Exact-band patching suggested the upper band can act as a semantic row-context anchor, coordinate-height prior, or disambiguator for stacked same-desc books. The hard y2 extent contrast preserved the failure surface: target `y2` bins remained low-ranked with top1 far from the target under several conditions.

The auxiliary chair same-slot probe was a useful negative. It did not justify more shallow aux same-slot patching and redirected attention to hard book rows and stronger hidden-state/context-band targets.

## Cross-Checkpoint And Selector Results

The June 12 cross-checkpoint decode panel regenerated aux manifests and compared parent/no-aligner/aux behavior under fixed decode settings. The highest-leverage hidden-state targets remained rows where `desc_x1` failed but adding `y1` or `x2` succeeded. The probe-family and transition selectors made this operational by separating same-slot patch targets, cross-slot guardrails, hard no-rescue rows, and coordinate-slot unlock rows.

The case panel showed that the vase remains qualitatively distinct: bad full-prefix baseline rank, wrong vertical top1 basin, large MLP patch recovery, and a layered direction signature. The book/chair rows are more context-dependent and less cleanly solved by moving one residual vector.

## Consolidated Findings

- Many valid FNs are recoverable or partially explainable by prefix/coordinate guidance; they should not be collapsed into visual invisibility.
- Correct `x1` can be an instance-binding seed, but wrong-control `x1` remains a leakage/control risk that must stay in decode panels.
- The vase case demonstrates same-slot prefix-state lock: full prefix can install a wrong coordinate basin before generation, and late MLP/residual patches can repair it.
- Book failures require broader context-band reasoning. Single visual-token patching was negative; context-ring and exact-band interventions are more promising.
- Auxiliary checkpoint FN behavior differs from parent behavior: it has more invalid/parse-blocked cases and more proxy/language evidence among valid FNs, so decode success rates must be interpreted with validity counters.

## Decisions And Interpretation Boundaries

- Keep same-slot hidden-state patching as the clean intervention for the vase-like class.
- Keep context-band/ring interventions separate from single-token visual patching for stacked same-desc objects such as books.
- Do not interpret `desc_x1` rescue as a full visual proof; it is evidence that the conditional generative path exists under a coordinate seed.
- Do not continue shallow aux chair same-slot probes until a more targeted hidden-state or context-band hypothesis is available.

## Unresolved Questions

- Which FN rows generalize the vase same-slot repair mechanism beyond a single object and checkpoint?
- In hard book rows, is the upper context band acting semantically, geometrically, or through same-desc disambiguation?
- Can transition selectors predict which coordinate slot unlocks a missing object before running full decode panels?
- How should invalid/parse-blocked aux rows be folded into FN interpretability without overstating valid-case proxy evidence?

## Current Status

The FN branch has split into two practical families. The first is recoverable coordinate-basin lock, represented by the vase: same-slot late residual/MLP state can repair a wrong next-coordinate basin. The second is context-dependent hard FN, represented by books/chair controls: single visual-token or shallow same-slot interventions are insufficient, and broader context-band probes are the next useful surface.

## Next Steps

- Run same-slot patch panels on additional selector-chosen FN cases that match the vase transition class.
- For hard book rows, compare upper/context, target-adjacent, and neighbor/lower band patches across more stacked same-desc examples.
- Keep wrong-control coordinate guidance in every decode panel to measure coordinate leakage.
- Update this synthesis rather than adding one note per FN microprobe unless a new FN family appears.
