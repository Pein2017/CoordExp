# Autoregressive Duplication Causal Chain Synthesis

## Overview

This document consolidates the June 10-11 autoregressive duplication mechanism notes into one chronological mechanism narrative. The investigation began with a mechanism-first charter, then moved through residual patching, decoder-layer boundary sweeps, Layer 17 attention localization, visual-basin source decomposition, candidate-basin specificity checks, and x2 overcorrection controls. The strongest current claim is not that duplication comes from a generic late residual failure. The best-supported chain is a site-sensitive visual coordinate-basin route: selected visual regions change attention/value contributions, Layer 17 head 1 is the clearest causal routing/value site for the record-33 anchor, and later residual states carry or sharpen the coordinate-token basin.

The source fragments used many small reports because each probe produced one artifact. This synthesis removes repeated launch/status text and keeps the unique results, metrics, failure cases, and decision boundaries.

## Evidence Standard And Anchor Panel

The June 10 charter set the evidence standard: diagnose emitted duplicates, invalid rows, empty predictions, and false negatives as mechanisms only when a temporal event, hidden/logit/attention state, and causal intervention can be tied together. It required a joint parent/continuation ledger rather than separate ledgers, retained rows with parse failures or no burst evidence, and defined `primary_burst_onset_row` as the first repeat row of the final dominant duplicate component. The initial anchor cases were records `54`, `33`, `47`, `82`, and `96`, with record `33` becoming the strongest causal anchor for the Layer 17/head-1 branch.

The durable artifact root for the June 10-11 causal chain was usually `/data/CoordExp/outputs/analysis/autoregressive_duplication_mechanism/phase1_hidden_logit_manifest_20260610-073920`. Most probes used `none_latest_ckpt32` and `aux_latest_ckpt32`, with parent checkpoints used to separate inherited coordinate-token behavior from continuation-specific effects.

## Chronological Mechanism Narrative

### 1. Residual State Was Real But Too Coarse

The first broad residual-patch panels showed that coordinate-slot state could be moved bidirectionally. For `none_latest_ckpt32|post_y1/pre_x2`, late residual patches at layers `20` and `24` produced probability repair around `0.0146-0.0148` and comparable control-to-masked damage around `0.0133-0.0139`. That made the residual basin a real causal object, but it did not identify the origin.

Module-site probes then narrowed the boundary. Full decoder-layer patches were strongest at layers `20/24`; MLP input and output were equivalent at the selected token; isolated self-attention at those later layers was weak or counterproductive for repair. This redirected the question away from "which late MLP writes the basin" and toward "where was the residual state written before layer 20."

### 2. The Transition Localized To Layers 16-18, Then To Layer 17

Decoder input/output sweeps showed layer `12` was weak, layer `16` was a transition/asymmetric repair point, and layers `18+` carried the full bidirectional residual basin. The fine sweep around layers `14-18` sharpened the story: a repair-capable direction was already readable by `14-16`, but the full bidirectional basin emerged by `17-18`. Layer `16` attention head `13` remained a plausible contributor, but grouped attention patches did not explain the full repair route.

Layer 17 became the focal boundary because self-attention output at that layer was the first site that looked like the coordinate-basin transition rather than a carried late residual state. Layer 17 module-boundary probes argued against a simple MLP-origin story: before self-attention the state was repair-capable but asymmetric; after self-attention it became much closer to the downstream basin.

### 3. Layer 17 Head 1 Was The Core Route For The Strongest Anchor

All-head and head-level probes identified Layer 17 head 1 as the key attention head for the primary `none_latest_ckpt32`, record `33`, `post_y1/pre_x2` anchor. Adding heads `3` or `7` slightly improved repair in some group probes, but singleton head 1 captured the main effect and larger groups could dilute it. The old layer-16 head-13 hypothesis became an upstream contributor, not the final causal head.

Projected-contribution and value-source patches then made the claim sharper. Patching the pre-`o_proj` source contribution from the duplicate basin was causally sufficient; the projected contribution survived `o_proj`; and injecting the projected duplicate-basin vector at `self_attn_output` nearly matched the pre-`o_proj` source-contribution repair. The same projected vector failed when injected after attention residual construction or after post-attention norm. That site sensitivity is important: the mechanism is not just "some vector in residual space," it has to enter through the attention output path.

### 4. The Source Was Visual And Local, Not A Previous-Coordinate Copy

Source-category readouts ruled out the tempting explanation that Layer 17 head 1 mainly copied previous coordinate tokens. At the primary `y1 -> x2` onset, it was primarily a visual-source head. Source-region controls showed the duplicate-basin visual cell collapsed from mean mass `0.427491` to `0.005393` under masking while rest-of-image mass stayed essentially unchanged (`0.953100` to `0.955183`). Value-source and visual-spatial bucket probes then showed that the useful positive contribution was local to the duplicate visual basin, while a near-ring visual bucket carried much of the opposing direction.

The component split also revealed cancellation: duplicate-basin components repaired masked-to-control behavior, while non-region/visual-non-basin components often supported the opposite control-to-masked direction. Whole-head deltas could be smaller than either component because the head carried competing coordinate directions.

### 5. Candidate-Basin Specificity Split The Mechanism Into Gates

June 11 candidate probes showed the mechanism was more specific than "local visual attention helps." Record 33's original duplicate basin was a clean aligned route-and-value case. Some target-local or target-desc candidates had strong route or Q/K mass but weak or negative value contribution. The resulting taxonomy was: aligned route-and-value, route-without-value, value-without-clean-route, and destructive local pockets.

Fine-spatial probes on records `50` and `114` showed that candidate basins can contain sign-flipping subregions. Parent candidate regions could be harmful while margin-scale subregions around the same candidate split into positive and negative directions. This is why the best current read is a structured coordinate-slot attraction field, not a homogeneous visual-region value source.

### 6. X2 Overcorrection Was A Related But Distinct Failure Mode

The x2 overcorrection branch isolated an auxiliary-checkpoint failure surface: masked language-side state could already be at the correct x2 coordinate, while whole-head restoration overcorrected into a lower-coordinate attractor and reduced target-near mass. Score-bias restoration repaired route distributions but was usually not sufficient for coordinate movement. Key-state restoration was stronger than score-only, key+value was stronger still, and downstream residual patches showed low-side correction beginning around layer `16` and sharpening later. The auxiliary checkpoint therefore did not simply "lack control movement"; it often moved too decisively into a lower control basin.

Cross-checkpoint direction panels separated at least three regimes: balanced relocation (`none_latest_ckpt32` and no-aligner parent record 48), lower-control attraction (`aux_latest_ckpt32`), and destructive or mixed late residual trajectories. This means a fix must distinguish useful downward correction from auxiliary overcorrection.

## Consolidated Findings

- The strongest duplicate mechanism evidence is the record `33`, `none_latest_ckpt32`, `post_y1/pre_x2` chain: duplicate-basin masking changes Layer 17/head-1 visual attention, value contribution, projected head output, and downstream coordinate logits.
- Layer `17`, head `1`, `self_attn_output` is the clearest causal boundary for the primary branch. Later residual layers carry the basin; they are not the origin.
- The mechanism is visual-local, not previous-coordinate copying. It depends on small visual basins and near-ring/non-basin opponents.
- `o_proj` preserves and scales an already meaningful head-local contribution rather than creating the basin from noise.
- Candidate quality has at least three gates: route selection, value direction, and downstream residual compatibility. Passing one gate is not enough.
- Auxiliary x2 overcorrection is related to duplicate-basin routing but differs in sign and target-neighborhood movement; it needs separate controls.

## Decisions And Interpretation Boundaries

- Keep record `33` as the primary causal anchor for Layer 17/head-1 mechanism claims.
- Do not claim a single universal duplication circuit. Parent, none-continuation, and auxiliary checkpoints show different directional regimes.
- Do not promote route-only, score-only, or Q/K-only probes as sufficient. They are useful localizers but need value/residual outcome checks.
- Treat full decoder-layer/residual patch results as state-causality evidence, not source-origin evidence, unless paired with source/content decomposition.
- Preserve the FN branch separately because FN guidance shares coordinate-basin phenomena but diagnoses missing-object recovery rather than duplicate onset.

## Unresolved Questions

- Does Layer 16 head 8/13 activity feed Layer 17/head-1 behavior through a stable bridge, or are these separate cases with similar coordinate-basin geometry?
- Can the candidate-gate taxonomy predict when a basin is constructive, route-only, value-only, or destructive before running a causal patch?
- Which source tokens define the near-ring opponent direction, and can that opponent be exploited to suppress duplicate basins without hurting true positives?
- Is x2 overcorrection a checkpoint-specific calibration artifact or a general failure mode whenever low-side correction is too strong?

## Current Status

The June 10-11 duplication branch has a coherent causal story: visual-local duplicate-basin routing through Layer 17/head 1 can install or repair a coordinate-token basin at the attention-output boundary, while later residual layers carry and sometimes sharpen that basin. Candidate and x2 follow-ups show the mechanism is gated and sign-sensitive, not a monolithic "more visual attention is better" phenomenon.

## Next Steps

- Run candidate-gate prediction on additional records before causal patching, then check whether route/value/residual compatibility predicts repair sign.
- For x2 overcorrection, separate useful downward correction from harmful lower-anchor attraction with matched rows across `none`, `aux`, and parent checkpoints.
- Test whether near-ring or visual-non-basin opponent directions can suppress duplicate-basin attraction without damaging target-local value signals.
- Keep future reports as additions to this synthesis unless a new mechanism branch clearly separates from Layer 17/head-1 visual basin routing.
